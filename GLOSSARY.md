# Core Values

Core Values supports reflection on personal priorities and how those priorities show up in everyday choices. These terms
describe the agreed domain language, including the proposed Putting Values into Practice feature.

## Language

**Core value**: A priority that a person identifies as important to them and can use to reflect on their choices.

**Team values exercise**: A facilitated group exercise for a team meeting. The team uses the team-focused value cards to
make choices together, discuss the trade-offs and agree which values they want to guide how they work. The app provides
the card-sorting structure for the conversation; it does not combine separate individual results or provide a shared
online workspace.

**Finish option**: A choice to end the current sorting round and complete the selected set using the highest-ranked
values available. _Avoid_: Early Finish as a user-facing action label

**Putting Values into Practice**: A reflection exercise exploring the relationship between a person's values, choices,
motivations and possible effects on others. _Avoid_: Behaviour assessment, personality test

**Reflection**: One exploration of a selection of situations and the person's responses to them, which they may leave
and return to. Its summary concerns the responses within that reflection.

**Life context**: An area of everyday life in which a situation occurs, such as work, home, relationships or community.

**Situation**: A circumstance inviting someone to consider how they might respond. It may be prepared for the exercise
or brought by the person from their own life.

**Reflection area**: A kind of everyday choice explored by a situation, such as offering support or sharing
responsibility. It organises situations within a life context.

**Value relevance**: A curated reason why a situation could help someone explore a value. It supports suggestions
without establishing how that person's response connects to the value.

**Hypothetical response**: What a person says they would be likely to do in a situation they are considering. _Avoid_:
Observed behaviour

**Real example**: A person's account of something they actually did in a situation they experienced.

**Motivation**: What the person says mattered to them or what they hoped to achieve when responding to a situation.

**Value connection**: A link the person makes between a response and a value that matters to them. They may experience
that relationship as aligned, in tension or uncertain.

**Recurring theme**: A tentative similarity across a person's responses to distinct situations within a reflection.
_Avoid_: Personality type, trait score

**Possible perspective**: A hypothetical way another person might experience or interpret an action. _Avoid_: Actual
opinion, received feedback

**Received feedback**: Something the person reports that another person actually communicated about their actions or
impact.

**Action to try**: A small action the person chooses after reflecting on their responses and values.
