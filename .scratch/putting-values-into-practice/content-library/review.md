**Editorial note:** Neither a simple gift nor a more elaborate one establishes how much a person cares.

#### Two versions of a shared memory

- **RESOURCEFULNESS** (`team:34`): Explore how to use the people and time still available.

## Optional examples for later reflection

These support the shared deeper prompts; they must not be assigned as conclusions about a person.

### Offering support

**Where this could help:** Your attention, experience or practical help could make a difficult situation easier.

**A possible cost:** Help could arrive before the other person has had room to say what they need.

**Possible perspectives:** Someone might feel supported; someone else might want more room to decide for themselves.

**Actions someone could choose:**

- Ask what kind of support would help before offering it.
- Agree one useful contribution and leave room for the other person's approach.

### Sharing responsibility

**Where this could help:** Following through and making ownership clear could help people rely on one another.

**A possible cost:** Taking on more could leave less room for other people to contribute or for your own commitments.

**Possible perspectives:** Someone might appreciate your reliability; someone else might be unsure what remains theirs to do.

**Actions someone could choose:**

- Agree who owns the next step and what help is available.
- Check whether stepping in now will make future responsibility clearer.

### Speaking openly

**Where this could help:** A concern, preference or different view could help others understand what matters.

**A possible cost:** Timing or delivery could make it harder for someone to hear the point; waiting could also leave it unheard.

**Possible perspectives:** Someone might welcome your candour; someone else might need more context or a different moment.

**Actions someone could choose:**

- Explain one concern and ask what you may be missing.
- Choose a time to express a preference you have been leaving unspoken.

### Working to a standard

**Where this could help:** Care and attention could make something more useful, dependable or satisfying.

**A possible cost:** Further refinement could consume time or reduce room for another person's way of doing things.

**Possible perspectives:** Someone might value the care; someone else might be uncertain when the work will be finished.

**Actions someone could choose:**

- Agree what good enough means for this task before starting.
- Ask which details matter to the person who will use the result.

### Making room for your needs

**Where this could help:** Clear limits and requests for help could make commitments more sustainable.

**A possible cost:** An unspoken limit could become resentment; a boundary without context could be hard for others to understand.

**Possible perspectives:** Someone might appreciate knowing what you can offer; someone else might need time to adjust their expectations.

**Actions someone could choose:**

- Say what you can offer and what you need to protect.
- Ask for one specific change or piece of help.

### Responding to uncertainty

**Where this could help:** Questions, provisional decisions or small trials could make progress possible with incomplete information.

**A possible cost:** Waiting could delay a useful step; moving quickly could make an assumption harder to revisit.

**Possible perspectives:** Someone might appreciate your caution or momentum; someone else might need the assumptions made clearer.

**Actions someone could choose:**

- Name one assumption and agree when to review it.
- Choose a small next step that leaves room to change direction.
